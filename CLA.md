# Contributor Licence Agreement

**This is a draft. It has not been reviewed by a lawyer, and it is the one file in this repository where that matters.** Read it as a statement of intent until that review has happened; if you are contributing something substantial, say so in your pull request and we will sort the paperwork out first.

By contributing to Valey you agree to the following.

## 1. You wrote it

The contribution is your original work, or you have the right to submit it. If your employer has rights to what you write, you have their permission. You are not knowingly submitting anyone else's code, and where you have adapted something, you say so in the pull request.

## 2. You keep it

You keep the copyright in your contribution. Nothing here transfers ownership.

## 3. What you grant

You grant Sergei Goriugin (xoyk), the author of Valey, a worldwide, royalty-free, irrevocable, non-exclusive licence to use, reproduce, modify, distribute and sublicense your contribution, under the AGPL-3.0 and **under other licence terms as well**, including proprietary ones.

You also grant the same people a licence to any patents you hold that your contribution necessarily infringes, for the purpose of using it in the project.

## 4. Why clause 3 exists, in plain words

Valey is open core. The core is AGPL-3.0 for everybody, and some modules are sold. Without this grant, a patch to the open core could not be carried into a paid module — so the project would have to refuse patches exactly where they help most, or quietly rewrite them, which is worse.

This is a real asymmetry and it is not disguised: your contribution can end up in something sold, and your name stays on it in the history. If that is not a trade you want to make, an issue that describes a bug precisely is genuinely valuable and asks nothing of you.

## 5. No warranty

You provide your contribution as is, without warranties of any kind, to the extent the law allows.

## How to sign

Add a `Signed-off-by:` line to your commits with your real name and an email you read:

```
Signed-off-by: Jane Developer <jane@example.com>
```

`git commit -s` writes it for you. That line means you have read this file and agree to it for that contribution.
