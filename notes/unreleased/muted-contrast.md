The small print of the office — key hints, dates, counters, the role and branch under a name, the version under the entrance — was set in two dim tones, and neither was readable enough: 2.2 to 4.5 to 1 against the panels they sit on, where 4.5 is the usual floor for text. It was worst exactly where it is read in passing.

Now all of it is one tone, a step lighter, and it clears 4.5 to 1 on every panel surface in each of the six office colours — 5 to 1 in the darkest, night — and on any hue the colour panel can turn to. The second, dimmer tone is kept for borders and rules only: two dim tones that both pass cannot be told apart anyway. A key hint on a button takes the brighter text tone instead, because no dim tone reads on a button's face. What is switched off stays faint on purpose: keys that do nothing on the current screen, and the entrance menu while you are out in the corridor.

The price is a little hierarchy: a note after a line, like «· no report» on the standup, is now the same colour as the line itself.
