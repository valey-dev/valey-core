---
title: veterans' desks
scope: office
---

Every desk in the office looked the same whether its agent had arrived an hour ago or had been talking with you for a week. The mug and the sheet of paper on it were handed out by the place, not by whoever sat there.

Now a desk slowly fills up. An agent whose conversation is a day old and holds a hundred replies gets a thing on the monitor — a rubber duck, a bobblehead or a snow globe; three days and three hundred add one at the left end, a week and eight hundred one at the right, where the cup lives. Both conditions count at every rung, so a chat left open and forgotten stays bare, and so does yesterday's chatterbox. The set is the agent's own, drawn from its id: the same veteran always keeps the same duck. Age is counted from the first reply in the transcript rather than from when the process started, so restarting the app strips nobody. What was left out on purpose: no toast, no counter, no line explaining any of it — the desk just becomes somebody's, and whoever notices, notices.
