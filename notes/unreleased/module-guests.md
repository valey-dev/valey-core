---
title: A module says whether a guest may see it
scope: modules
---

An invitation was all or nothing. `admitted()` asks whether you were invited and stops there, so a guest called in to watch the agents work also loaded the easel with unreleased designs, the git tree with branch names, and the filing cabinet of personal files. Nothing was hidden because nothing had been asked to hide.

The manifest decides now. `"guests": "shown"` and nothing else counts as yes: a module that forgets the line is the owner's alone. Silence must not open — the same reasoning that makes the lists in `.gitignore` named rather than «everything not ours», and it means a module added tomorrow is private until somebody says otherwise. `moduleList()` and `moduleRoute()` both default to the guest's view, so a caller who forgets the argument gets less than the truth, never more.

Two gates, not one. The filtered list keeps a hidden module's client off the page, so its keys, objects and panels never exist for a guest at all; and the route is not offered the request either, because hiding the list alone is theatre when a URL can be typed by hand. A hidden module is skipped rather than refused, so a guest cannot tell one from a module that was never installed — `/api/wip` answers 404 to them and 200 to the owner.

Ownership is resolved at most once per request, and only when a hidden module is actually in the way. The office's `isOwner` is handed to module routes as a function precisely because most of them never ask and asking costs a read of the settings; an office whose modules are all open to guests still reads nothing here.

The two free modules declare themselves shown — navigation and music, nothing to hide. Every paid module is hidden until its own manifest says otherwise, and that is the owner's call in their own repository.

**No frames here, and that is a gap rather than a choice.** The difference this makes is two offices side by side — the same key shelf with and without the Figma card — and a shot recipe photographs the owner's demo office only. There is no way to declare a guest in a recipe, so the pair was checked by hand on a stand and could not be committed.
